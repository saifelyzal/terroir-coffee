What You'll Need

The kit

Four pieces of gear, none of them complicated. What matters is control — over temperature, over pour rate, over time.

Dripper
A V60 or Chemex — anything with a cone or fluted bed that lets water draw through evenly.
Filter
Paper, rinsed with hot water before brewing to clear papery taste and pre-warm the vessel.
Gooseneck Kettle
Gives you a thin, controllable stream — the difference between an even bed and a channeled one.
Scale & Timer
Pour over is measured, not eyeballed. Weigh the coffee, weigh the water, time the bloom.
Method

Six steps, three minutes

20g of coffee, ground medium-coarse — like coarse sand. 300g of water total, just off the boil. Follow the pours in order; the timing matters more than precision to the second.

01
Rinse & Dose
Rinse the filter, discard the runoff, add 20g of ground coffee and level the bed.
0:00
02
Bloom
Pour 40g of water to fully saturate the grounds. Swirl once, then let the coffee de-gas.
0:00 – 0:45
03
First Pour
Slow spiral from the center out, up to 150g total. Keep the bed level, avoid the filter's edge.
0:45 – 1:15
04
Second Pour
Continue in spirals to 300g total, keeping the water level roughly constant as it drains.
1:15 – 1:45
05
Drawdown
Let gravity finish the job. A flat, evenly-drained bed is the sign of a clean extraction.
1:45 – 3:00
06
Serve
Swirl the carafe to even out the brew, pour, and drink while it's still hot enough to steam.

Troubleshooting

Tasting it right

Pour over is forgiving once you know what the flavor is telling you. The most common misses, and the one-variable fix for each.

Tastes sharp or sour
Under-extracted. Grind slightly finer, or slow your pour rate so water spends longer in contact with the grounds.
Tastes flat or bitter
Over-extracted. Grind slightly coarser, or shorten the drawdown by pouring in fewer, faster stages.
Water pools, drains unevenly
Channeling. Pour in tighter spirals and keep the stream off the filter's paper edge.
Brew finishes too fast
Grind too coarse for the dripper, or too little coffee for the bed depth. Increase dose or grind finer.

Every cup is a small experiment. Change one variable at a time, and let the coffee tell you what's next.
